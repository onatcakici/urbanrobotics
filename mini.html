Mini, up close

Mini is the small one, with two arms for light technician tasks: swapping parts, pressing buttons, plugging in cables and checking equipment. Mini is in design. Everything on this page comes from the CAD model.

Inside

A round drive base that turns in its own footprint, a torso with an arm at each shoulder, and a head with a depth camera and the E stop. The base carries the battery and the computer, and a small shelf at the back holds parts.

  1. Two arms. AgileX PiPER six axis arms, one at each shoulder: 1.5 kg (3.3 lb) payload and 626 mm (24.6 in) reach each. Stowed, they hang at the sides, inside the outline of the base.
  2. Grippers. A PiPER parallel gripper on each arm, opening to 70 mm (2.8 in), with a small camera on each for close work.
  3. Head camera. A Luxonis OAK D Lite stereo depth camera behind the window in the head, tilted 15 degrees down so it sees both hands.
  4. E stop. On top of the head, where it can be reached from every side.
  5. Lidar. A Slamtec RPLIDAR C1 behind the slot in the front of the base, for mapping and finding the way.
  6. Computer. An NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super. Each arm talks to it over CAN through its own USB adapter.
  7. Battery. A Bioenno LiFePO4 pack, 24 V and 30 Ah (720 Wh), laid flat in the back of the base.
  8. Drive. Two ODrive BotWheel hub motor wheels, with casters front and back, so Mini turns in its own footprint.
