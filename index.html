Drop it and leave.

P2 is a painting robot for jobsites and homes. Roll it in, pour the paint and lock up: it is designed to paint the walls and ceiling on its own, overnight or while you are out, and to send a report when the job is done.

P2 has not been built yet. The images are CAD renderings, and the numbers come from our simulations. We are building the first bench rig in Miami, and during pilots our team will stay on site.

How P2 works

Three steps and about 1.4 hours of your time for a bedroom, taping included. P2 is designed to do the rest on its own.

  1. Drop

    Move the furniture about 1 m away from the walls, tape the trim and the ceiling line and lay the paper runner, as you would for a painter. Roll P2 in. One charge is designed to last the whole room.

  2. Load

    Pour the paint into P2’s sealed tank. P2 maps the room with its LiDAR and cameras, finds the walls, trim, outlets, doors and windows, and tells you when it will be done. If something is still against a wall, it asks before it starts.

  3. Leave

    Lock up and go. P2 paints, closes a cover over its roller while each coat dries, and sends the job report when it is done. When you are back, pull the tape and put the furniture back.

At the wall

At each spot along the wall, P2 lowers two feet, cuts the ceiling line, the baseboard line, the corners and the trim with an edger pad on guide wheels, then rolls the wall in lanes from floor to ceiling. Its camera checks each panel before it moves on. After the last coat it pumps the paint in its line back to the tank, and parks by the door or rolls on to the next room.

This is how P2 is designed to work. In our simulation of a 12 by 12 ft bedroom with an 8 ft ceiling, P2 needs 2.6 hours of work and 5.1 hours from start to finish, including two hours of drying between coats.

On a jobsite

Your crew clocks out. P2 clocks in.

  • Apartment units, hotel rooms and offices: walls and ceilings up to 9 ft.
  • A job report per room for the super, done before the crew is back.
  • A fleet number on every P2, so the report says which robot did which unit.

At home

Leave for the night, or for the day.

  • No crew in your house all day. The furniture stays in the room, moved 1 m from the walls.
  • The same report lands on your phone, with a photo of every wall.

Why P2

Most of what you pay for a paint job is labor: 75 to 95 percent of a professional interior job, according to Angi. P2 is built to take that labor off the bill.

Specs

Needs a 30 inch doorway

To travel, P2 lowers its mast and folds its arm against it. Stowed, it is 1.48 m tall, 0.68 m wide and 0.78 m long, so it needs a doorway at least 30 in wide and 80 in tall, the common size for bedroom doors. At the wall, the mast lifts the arm until the roller reaches a 9 ft ceiling.

Built for paint and repair

Standard wear parts
The roller cover, the edger pad and the pump hose are stock parts that anyone can replace.
Locks that hold without power
The Tool Port keeps its tool and the lift brakes hold the mast when the power is off.
Made for paint
The mast runs on V wheels with felt wipers instead of ball rails. Paint only touches the sealed tank, the hose and the tool.
Modules, not one block
The mast comes off the base with one lever, and every tool shares one port.
Failure modes first
We listed 43 ways P2 could fail, from a drip to a drill hitting a wire, and gave each one a design answer and a test.

Paint first

Painting is the only tool we are building. The arm and the Tool Port are made so that later tools can fit the same robot, and we have drawn a few of them in CAD to test the idea. They are concepts, not products: none is built, and which ones come next will depend on what pilot customers need.

Tool on the Tool Port

In development

Rolls walls and ceilings in lanes, and cuts the lines at the ceiling, the corners and the trim.

  • A 9 in roller fed with paint through its core.
  • An edger pad on guide wheels for the cut lines.
  • A cover that closes over the roller while each coat dries.
  • A camera and a color sensor that check every panel.

Concept, not built

Sprays empty rooms and new builds, where there is nothing to cover.

  • An airless spray gun with a reversible tip.
  • A distance sensor that holds the gun steady off the wall.
  • A tank and a high pressure pump that ride in the base.

Concept, not built

Sands filled patches smooth between coats.

  • A 125 mm random orbit sander.
  • A brush skirt around the pad.
  • Its own fan and HEPA dust canister.

Concept, not built

Drills anchor holes where you mark them, for shelves, TVs and curtain rods.

  • A stud and live wire scanner that checks behind the wall first.
  • A clear dust cup on guide rods.
  • Depth control for every hole.

Concept, not built

Tapes the trim and the ceiling line before painting, and pulls the tape after the last coat.

  • A 48 mm roll of painter’s tape.
  • A guide wheel that rides the edge of the trim.
  • A pressure roller and a cutter.
  • A folded pull tab at each end, so peeling takes one grab.

Concept, not built

Tapes the drywall seams and spreads the joint compound, one coat a night.

  • Self adhesive mesh tape laid along each seam.
  • A 250 mm flat box that leaves a smooth, feathered coat.
  • Compound pumped from a hopper in the base.
  • A camera that finds the seams and the screw heads.

Concept, not built

Sets wall tile for backsplashes and bathroom walls.

  • A suction pad for tiles up to 300 by 300 mm.
  • Adhesive from a cartridge, combed on by a notched nozzle.
  • A cross line laser and a camera that keep the joints straight.
  • A rack on the deck that carries the tiles.

Concept, not built

Prints murals, logos and patterns straight onto the wall.

  • Four color inkjet heads.
  • An LED bar that cures the ink as it prints.
  • Guide wheels that hold the heads a few millimeters off the wall.
  • Ink cartridges on the tool.

One Tool Port. Every tool starts with the same tool plate. Six spring loaded balls lock it to the Tool Port and stay locked when power is off; a solenoid opens them only when you release the tool from the app. Two dowels locate the tool, eight pins carry power and data, and a flat face coupling passes paint without a drip.

Safety

  • P2 is designed to pause when a person or pet comes close. Its cameras watch the work area, the arm slows and stops near people, and the arm itself is force limited.
  • Two stabilizer feet go down before the mast rises. In the worst case we simulated, an empty tank with the arm stopped dead at full reach and the mast at the top, P2 still keeps 82 mm of tip margin.
  • An emergency stop on the robot and in the app cuts motor power through a hardwired safety relay.
  • P2 uses water based paints only. Open a window while it works.
  • P2 does not sand or scrape in its first version. In homes built before 1978, work that disturbs old paint must follow the EPA lead safe rules.
  • P2 uses its cameras to navigate and to check the paint. Before a pilot, we agree with you on what is recorded and when it is deleted.
  • P2 is in development and has no safety certification. During pilots, our team will stay on site.

Questions

Who is it for?
Builders and painting contractors who want rooms painted while their crew works elsewhere, property managers who repaint units between tenants, and homeowners who want a room painted without a crew in the house all day.
What will it cost?
We have not set a price, and P2 is not for sale yet. Our goal is a price per room below what a painting crew charges. A home is repainted every few years, so for homes we expect to offer P2 as a service or a rental rather than a machine to buy.
When can I use it?
Not yet. Next we build a bench rig to test the paint tool on real walls, then the full robot, then pilot jobs in South Florida.
What paint does it use?
Water based interior paint, the same cans a painter buys.
Does someone need to be there?
During pilots, yes: our team stays on site. P2 itself is designed to work on its own and to ask for help on your phone when it needs it.
How high can it reach?
The first version paints rooms up to 9 ft. Ten foot rooms are a later goal; the current design reaches them only in part.

Talk to us

Build, paint or manage apartments in South Florida? Help us plan the first pilot jobs. Homeowners are welcome too. It starts with a 15 minute call about how you repaint today: what it costs, how long it takes and what slows it down. Nothing is for sale yet.
