Paints, drills, sands, and more.

P3 is one robot for the light, repetitive work inside a building. Its arm picks its own tools from the deck: a paint fed roller, a sprayer, a sander, a drill, a gripper and a taping head. A three stage mast lifts it to 16 ft walls.

P3 has not been built yet. The images are CAD renderings, and the numbers come from our simulations. We are building the first bench rig in Miami, and during pilots our team will stay on site.

How it works

  1. Scan

    P3 maps the room with its lidar and cameras and finds every wall, corner, outlet, door and window.

  2. Plan

    It plans the job from the scan and the drawings: lanes for paint, lines to cut in, holes to drill, patches to sand.

  3. Work

    It picks the right tool from its deck, does the work and swaps tools on its own, with force sensing at the wrist.

  4. Check

    A camera checks the result as it goes, and P3 fixes what it misses before it moves on.

  5. Report

    Photos of every wall and a list of what was done land on your phone.

Wall by wall

P3 parks once and rolls the wall in lanes from the baseboard up. Its swerve drive moves it sideways to the next section, and it cuts the ceiling line with the roller tilted into the corner while it drives. After the last coat, the paint in the line drains back to the tank.

Simulated in a 9 ft room: eight lanes from two stops, then one pass along the ceiling line.

Where it works

The same robot on a commercial fit out, a new apartment or a data hall: walls, ceilings and the small jobs between them.

On a jobsite

More rooms per painter.

  • Your crew preps the next unit while P3 works this one.
  • Each unit gets its own report, tagged with the robot that did it.

Up high

Nobody on a ladder.

  • The ceiling line is cut along the tape, with the roller tilted into the corner.
  • Anchors go into the ceiling from the deck, with the dust caught at the bit.
  • In a lived in home, the furniture stays in the room, pulled off the walls.

Why P3

Most of what you pay for a paint job is labor: 75 to 95 percent of a professional interior job, according to Angi. P3 is built to take that labor off the bill, and the same robot drills, sands and tapes too.

Lower cost
P3 does the slow, repetitive part of the job. Our goal is a price per room below what a crew charges.
No overspray
A roller puts no mist in the air, so there is nothing to wrap in plastic.
No trips to the tray
A pump feeds paint through the roller core from a sealed 10 L tank in the base, the same idea as the power rollers painters already use.
Nobody on a ladder
The mast does the reaching, up to 16 ft walls. Falls from ladders are among the common hazards of the trade.

Painters are hard to hire: about 24,900 openings a year in the U.S. through 2035. P3 is built to give each one more finished rooms a week.

Sources: NerdWallet, Cost to Paint a House in 2026, citing Angi; U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, Painters; Bob Vila, review of a Wagner power roller. P3 is in development.

Progress

  1. November 2026

    Designed P3, our first full product, in CAD: a swerve drive base, a three stage mast that stows under an 80 in door, a 7.5 kg collaborative arm and six tools it swaps on its own. Checked weight, tipping and reach, and animated a job from the CAD and the arm’s kinematics with every move checked for collisions.

  2. Before that

    Designed two earlier painting robots, a spray robot for commercial interiors and a roller robot for homes. Their base, mast and paint work carried into P3.

  3. Next

    Build a bench rig with the arm, the tool changer and the paint tool, and paint test walls to measure drips, lap marks and cut lines.

  4. After that

    Build the full robot, then run the first pilot jobs, on jobsites and in homes in South Florida.
