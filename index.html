How P2 works

Your part is the prep. The rest is designed to be P2’s.

  1. Prep

    Pull the furniture 1 m off the walls, tape the trim and the ceiling line, and lay the paper runner. The same prep a painter asks for.

  2. Load

    Pour the paint into the sealed tank. P2 maps the room with LiDAR and cameras, finds every wall, outlet, door and window, and gives you a finish time. Anything still against a wall gets flagged before it starts.

  3. Leave

    Go. P2 paints, covers its roller while each coat dries, and messages you when the room is done. Pull the tape and you are finished.

Room by room

At each stop, P2 sets two feet down, cuts the edges with a guided pad, then rolls the wall in lanes from floor to ceiling. A camera checks every panel before it moves on. After the last coat, the paint in the line drains back to the tank.

Simulated: a 12 by 12 ft bedroom with an 8 ft ceiling takes 2.6 hours of rolling and 5.1 hours start to finish, two hours of drying included.

Where it works

The same robot and the same prep, whether the room is a new unit or a lived in home.

On a jobsite

More rooms per painter.

  • Apartments, hotel rooms and offices, with walls and ceilings up to 9 ft.
  • Your painters prep the next unit while P2 rolls this one.
  • Each unit gets its own report, tagged with the robot that did it.

At home

No crew in the house all day.

  • The furniture stays in the room, pulled off the walls.
  • A photo of every wall lands on your phone.

Why P2

Most of what you pay for a paint job is labor: 75 to 95 percent of a professional interior job, according to Angi. P2 is built to take that labor off the bill.

1.4 h

of a person’s time per bedroom with P2, taping included, against about 6.5 hours for a painter by hand. Both are our estimates.

Lower cost
P2 does the slow part of the job, the rolling and the cutting in. Our goal is a price per room below what a painting crew charges.
No overspray
A roller puts no mist in the air, so there is nothing to wrap in plastic.
No trips to the tray
A pump feeds paint through the roller core from a sealed 6 gal tank, the same idea as the power rollers painters already use. One tank holds enough for about two bedrooms.
Nobody on a ladder
The mast does the reaching. Falls from ladders are among the common hazards of the trade.

Painters are hard to hire: about 24,900 openings a year in the U.S. through 2035. P2 is built to give each one more finished rooms a week.

Sources: NerdWallet, Cost to Paint a House in 2026, citing Angi; U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, Painters; Bob Vila, review of a Wagner power roller. Times and paint use for P2 come from our simulation; P2 is in development.

Progress

  1. October 2026

    Finished the CAD design of P2 and its paint tool, with concepts for later tools. Simulated a full bedroom, start to finish. Checked reach and stability: in the worst case P2 keeps 82 mm of tip margin. Listed 43 failure modes, each with a design answer and a test. Animated a job from the CAD and the arm’s kinematics.

  2. Before that

    Designed P1, a spray robot for commercial interiors. Its base and mast work carried into P2.

  3. Next

    Build a bench rig with the arm, the Tool Port and the paint tool, and paint test walls to measure drips, lap marks and cut lines.

  4. After that

    Build the full robot, then run the first pilot jobs, on jobsites and in homes in South Florida.
