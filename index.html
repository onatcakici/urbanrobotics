Robot technicians. Built for repetitive work.

Mobile robots that change their own tools to load machines, swap print plates, move parts and paint walls. We set up the whole job.

R2 keeps your machines running.

It drives to the machine that needs it, unloads the finished part and loads the next one from its cassettes. Between machines, it swaps print plates and moves parts between stations.

  1. UR12e arm, 51 in of reach
  2. Lift with 35 in of travel
  3. Two grippers on a quick changer
  4. Cassettes for raw stock and finished parts

R2 runs on the same base as R1.

How it works

Every job runs in four steps.

Scan
The robot maps the space and finds every machine, rack, wall and door it will work around.
Plan
It plans the job from the scan: parts to load, plates to swap, lanes to paint, holes to drill.
Work
It picks the right tool, does the work and checks the result with a camera as it goes.
Report
You get photos and a log of everything it did.

R1 does the same for walls.

It runs on the same base as R2, with a mast in place of the lift. Its arm picks its own tools from the deck, and the mast lifts it to the top of the wall. R1 rolls paint in lanes, cuts the ceiling line, drills, sands and tapes, then sends you photos of every wall.

  1. Paint roller, on the arm
  2. Drill
  3. Sander
  4. Tape head
  5. Gripper

The sprayer docks behind the mast.

One family, three jobs

R1 finishes walls in buildings. R2 tends machines in shops and print farms. Mini takes on light technician tasks with two arms. R1 and R2 share one chassis with swappable tops. Mini is the compact one.
