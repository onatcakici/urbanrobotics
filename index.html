Interior painting, done by a robot.

P2 is a painting robot for homes. It is designed to roll the walls and ceiling of a furnished room and cut every line on its own, with no masking and no mist.

P2 is in development. We are building the first prototype in Miami.

Why P2

Painting a room is slow, physical work. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics lists falls from ladders and muscle strains among the common hazards for painters, and projects about 24,900 openings for painters each year through 2035.

P2 is designed to paint the whole room: the ceiling, the walls and the lines where they meet. What is left for people is moving furniture away from the walls and putting it back.

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, Painters, Construction and Maintenance.

How it works

  1. Prep

    Move the furniture about 1 m away from the walls, lay the paper runner along them, and pour the paint into P2's sealed tank.

  2. Scan

    P2 maps the room with its LiDAR and cameras and finds the walls, trim, outlets, doors and windows. If something is still against a wall, it asks before it starts.

  3. Cut

    At each spot along the wall, P2 lowers two feet and cuts the ceiling line, the baseboard line, the corners and the trim with an edger pad that rides on guide wheels.

  4. Roll

    It rolls the wall in lanes from floor to ceiling, feeding paint through the roller core, and checks each panel with its camera before it moves on.

  5. Dry and finish

    Between coats P2 waits in its dock, charging, with the roller in a wet box. After the last coat it rinses its paint line and parks.

This is how P2 is designed to work. In our simulation of a 12 by 12 ft bedroom with an 8 ft ceiling, P2 needs 2.6 hours of work and 5.1 hours from start to finish, including two hours of drying between coats.

The machine

P2 is a round base with a two stage mast and a six axis arm. The base carries the paint, the pump and the battery; the mast lifts the arm; the arm holds the tool.

  1. 1
    LiDAR. Maps the room and finds walls, doors, trim and furniture.
  2. 2
    Two stage mast. Lifts the arm so the roller reaches from the baseboard to a 9 ft ceiling.
  3. 3
    Six axis arm. A collaborative arm with 0.7 m of reach and a force sensor at the wrist.
  4. 4
    Tool Port. Locks the tool with spring loaded balls that stay locked without power.
  5. 5
    Paint tool. A standard 9 in roller fed through its core, and an edger pad for the lines.
  6. 6
    Base. Carries a sealed 6 gal paint tank, the pump, the battery and two stabilizer feet.

Fits through any door

To travel, P2 lowers its mast and folds its arm over the base. At 1.48 m tall and 0.6 m across it fits under a standard 80 inch door and through a 30 inch doorway. At the wall, the mast lifts the arm until the roller reaches a 9 ft ceiling.

One arm, four tools

The arm can reach every point of a wall, so the tool on its wrist decides what P2 does. Paint comes first. Spray, Sand and Drill use the same Tool Port and are designed, not built yet. Pick a tool to see it change.

Tool on the Tool Port

Every tool starts with the same tool plate. Six spring loaded balls lock it to the Tool Port and stay locked when power is off; a solenoid opens them only when the tool sits in its bay at the dock. Two dowels locate the tool, eight pins carry power and data, and a flat face coupling passes paint without a drip.

P2 Dock

P2 backs in over two contact rails and charges through two pads under its base. The arm reaches the four tool bays behind it, sets one tool into its saddle, lets go, and picks up the next.

The paint bay has a wet box. Between coats the roller hangs inside it, so the cover stays wet for the next coat.

Built to work for years

  • Standard wear parts. The roller cover, the edger pad and the pump hose are stock parts that anyone can replace.
  • Locks that hold without power. The Tool Port keeps its tool and the lift brakes hold the mast when the power is off.
  • Made for paint. The mast runs on V wheels with felt wipers instead of ball rails. Paint only touches the sealed tank, the hose and the tool.
  • Modules, not one block. The mast comes off the base with one lever, and every tool shares one port.
  • Failure modes first. We listed 43 ways P2 could fail, from a drip to a drill hitting a wire, and gave each one a design answer and a test.

Safety

  • P2 is designed to pause when a person or pet comes close. Its cameras watch the work area, the arm slows and stops near people, and the arm itself is force limited.
  • Two stabilizer feet go down before the mast rises, and the arm's motion is limited when the tip margin gets small.
  • An emergency stop on the robot and in the app cuts motor power through a hardwired safety relay.
  • P2 uses water based paints only. Open a window while it works.
  • P2 does not sand or scrape in its first version. In homes built before 1978, work that disturbs old paint must follow the EPA lead safe rules.
  • P2 uses its cameras to navigate and to check the paint. Before a pilot, we agree with you on what is recorded and when it is deleted.
  • P2 is in development and has no safety certification. During pilots, our team will stay on site.
