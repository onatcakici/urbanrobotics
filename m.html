Ragnar M, up close

Ragnar M does the repetitive work in machine shops and print farms: it loads CNC machines, swaps print plates and moves parts between stations. It uses the same base as Ragnar C, with a lift and an arm on top instead of the mast.

How it works

Scan
Ragnar M maps the shop and finds every machine, rack and station it will work around.
Plan
It plans the job from the scan: parts to load, plates to swap, stations to visit.
Work
It picks the right gripper, does the work and checks the result with a camera as it goes.
Report
You get photos and a log of everything it did.

Inside

The same base as Ragnar C, with a lift and a collaborative arm with two grippers on top. The base carries the batteries, the computer and the depth cameras, and the deck carries a cassette for raw stock and one for finished parts.

  1. Arm. A Universal Robots UR12e: 27.6 lb (12.5 kg) payload and 51 in (1.3 m) reach, to load a machine through its door.
  2. Lift. An Ewellix LIFTKIT raises the arm up to 35 in (900 mm), from low fixtures to the top shelf of a print farm.
  3. Two grippers. OnRobot 2FG7 grippers on a dual quick changer, so it can hold a raw part and a finished part in one visit.
  4. Depth cameras. Four RealSense D435 on the corners of the base see all around it. A RealSense D405 on the wrist checks every grip up close.
  5. Computer. Two compute modules and the electronics, inside the base.
  6. Battery. Two battery packs, swapped through the rear door.
  7. Drive. Two swerve drive modules and four casters. It can drive sideways along a row of machines.
  8. Cassettes. One for raw stock and one for finished parts, on the deck.
