Ragnar M, up close

Ragnar M does the repetitive work in machine shops and print farms: it loads CNC machines, swaps print plates and moves parts between stations. It uses the same base as Ragnar C, with an arm on a fixed riser on top instead of the mast.

How it works

Set up
We prepare one machine: the door, the work holding and the cycle signal. We make the cassettes for your part.
Park
Ragnar M parks at the machine and finds the vise with its wrist camera.
Work
It unloads the finished part and loads the next one, cycle after cycle. Once one machine runs well, the same robot can cover the next.
Report
You get photos and a log of everything it did.

What it needs

Door
An automatic door, or one we fit
Work holding
An air vise or an air chuck
Signal
The machine tells the robot when a cycle ends
Reach
We check it on your machine before a pilot
Cycle time
Cycles of ten minutes or more suit it best, so one robot can cover more than one machine

Inside

The same base as Ragnar C, with a collaborative arm on a fixed riser on top. The base carries the battery, the computer, a lidar and a depth camera, and the deck carries a cassette for raw stock and one for finished parts.

  1. Arm. A Fairino FR5: 11 lb (5 kg) payload and 36.3 in (922 mm) reach, to load a machine through its door.
  2. Riser. A fixed steel riser holds the arm 33.5 in (850 mm) off the floor, so it reaches into a machine and down to its cassettes.
  3. Gripper. An electric parallel gripper. A RealSense D405 on the wrist checks every grip up close.
  4. Lidar and camera. A 2D lidar behind the glass band at the front stops it for obstacles. A RealSense D435 at the front corner lines it up at each machine.
  5. Computer. An NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super, the safety controller and the electronics, inside the base.
  6. Battery. One battery pack, swapped through the rear door.
  7. Drive. Two hub motor wheels and four casters.
  8. Cassettes. One for raw stock and one for finished parts, on the deck.
