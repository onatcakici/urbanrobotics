Robot technicians for buildings

We start with interior finishing. Ragnar Robotics is a robotics company in Miami building R1, one robot for the light, repetitive work inside a building: it paints, drills, sands and more, with tools it swaps on its own. R1 is in development: the CAD design is done, and bench tests on real walls come next.

Why paint

Painting is slow, physical work that every building needs: ladders, tape, plastic sheets and long days, and most of the bill is labor. Our first design was a spray robot for commercial interiors. Working on it showed us that most rooms are different: the furniture stays, and spray means masking everything. So R1 is designed to roll, the way a painter does, and to carry its tools on one arm: a roller, a sprayer, a sander, a drill, a gripper and a taping head, all on one tool changer.

How we work

Standard parts
R1 is designed around parts we can order: a collaborative arm, LiDAR, depth cameras and a standard 9 in roller. A repair should be a part swap, not a trip back to us.
Failure modes first
Before building, we list the ways R1 could fail, from a drip to a tip over, and give each a design answer and a test.
Numbers you can check
Every figure on this site is a design target, a simulation result or a sourced fact, and we say which.
Safety on site
R1 is designed to pause when a person or a pet comes close. During pilots, our team stays on site.

Where we are

  1. November 2026

    The CAD design of R1 is done: base, three stage mast, arm and six tools, with weight, tipping and reach checks and an animated job checked for collisions.

  2. Next

    A bench rig with the arm, the tool changer and the paint tool, painting test walls to measure drips, lap marks and cut lines.

  3. Then

    The full robot, and the first pilot jobs, on jobsites and in homes in South Florida.
