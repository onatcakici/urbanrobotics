Robot technicians for buildings

Ragnar Robotics is a robotics company in Miami that builds robot technicians: machines for the repetitive physical work inside buildings. We start with interior finishing. R1 paints, drills, sands and more, with tools it swaps on its own, and technicians for CNC machine shops and data centers come after it. R1 is in development: the CAD design is done, and bench tests on real walls come next.

Why paint first

Painting is slow, physical work that every building needs: ladders, tape, plastic sheets and long days, and most of the bill is labor. Our first design was a spray robot for commercial interiors. Working on it showed us that most rooms are different: the furniture stays, and spray means masking everything. So R1 is designed to roll, the way a painter does, and to carry its tools on one arm: a roller, a sprayer, a sander, a drill, a gripper and a taping head, all on one tool changer.

How we work

Standard parts
R1 is designed around parts we can order: a collaborative arm, LiDAR, depth cameras and a standard 9 in roller. A repair should be a part swap, not a trip back to us.
Failure modes first
Before building, we list the ways R1 could fail, from a drip to a tip over, and give each a design answer and a test.
Numbers you can check
Every figure on this site is a design target, a simulation result or a sourced fact, and we say which.
Safety on site
R1 is designed to pause when a person or a pet comes close. During pilots, our team stays on site.

Founder

Onat Cakici

Founder

LinkedIn

Onat leads the design of R1: the CAD model, the motion planning and the failure analysis. He is an aerospace engineer, with a B.S. from the Florida Institute of Technology (2025). He has built robots since he was 16: he designed competition robots with FIRST Robotics team Integra 3646, which qualified for the FIRST Championship twice, and helped build a telescoping arm that firefighters used to pull a trapped puppy from a well in Istanbul (Reuters, 2017). Before Ragnar, he led a nine engineer team that built an autonomous VTOL drone for early wildfire detection.
