We build robot technicians

Ragnar Robotics is a robotics company in Miami. We build mobile robots that change their own tools and take on the repetitive work in machine shops, print farms, data centers and buildings. R2 loads machines and swaps print plates. R1 paints, drills and sands walls. Both run on one base, and both are designed and simulated in CAD. Next, a real arm runs the first task, and the first pilots start in South Florida.

Why one base

A fixed robot arm pays off on a machine that runs all day. A small shop has many machines that each need a little loading every hour, plus plates to swap and parts to move. So our robots drive: one robot goes to whichever machine needs it, and changes tools for the other chores.

R1 and R2 share that base, with a different top for each job: a lift and an arm with two grippers for machines, a mast and six tools for walls. One base means one design to build, test and service, and every improvement to it reaches both robots.

How we work

Standard parts
Our robots are built from parts we can order: collaborative arms, lift columns, grippers, LiDAR and depth cameras. A repair should be a part swap, not a trip back to us.
Failure modes first
Before building, we list the ways a robot could fail, from a dropped part to a tip over, and give each a design answer and a test.
Numbers you can check
Every figure on this site is a design target, a simulation result or a sourced fact, and we say which.
Safety on site
Our robots are designed to pause when a person comes close. During pilots, our team stays on site.

Founder

Onat Cakici

Founder

LinkedIn

Onat leads the design of Ragnar's robots: the CAD models, the motion planning and the failure analysis. He is an aerospace engineer, with a B.S. from the Florida Institute of Technology (2025). He has built robots since he was 16: he designed competition robots with FIRST Robotics team Integra 3646, which qualified for the FIRST Championship twice, and helped build a telescoping arm that firefighters used to pull a trapped puppy from a well in Istanbul (Reuters, 2017). Before Ragnar, he led a nine engineer team that built an autonomous VTOL drone for early wildfire detection.
